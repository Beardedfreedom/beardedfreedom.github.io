# Skip Google Earth, Build From Free Lidar

Computationally, yes: anyone with a Google Maps Platform key can pull Google Earth's photogrammetric mesh as glTF tiles and land it in Blender at metre scale in an afternoon, and free tools (Blosm, 3dtiles-dl, Heron) exist to do exactly that. Contractually, no: the Google Maps Platform Terms of Service, fetched verbatim for this report (last modified August 26, 2026), prohibit exporting, storing or "creating content based on" Google Maps Content, and Google's own examples of forbidden conduct are building "3D building models" and "terrain models"; the Map Tiles API policies add that 3D objects may not be "extracted, traced, or otherwise derived by hand or machine from Photorealistic 3D Tiles"; and the 2026 Google Earth terms forbid downloading data layers at all. Having a Google Cloud account changes nothing except that it makes you a "Customer" whose project Google can suspend. What the YouTube videos actually show is either a paid plugin (Plex-Earth) sampling Google's 1–30 m elevation service into contours, or a RenderDoc GPU-capture hack that yields an un-georeferenced, non-commercial mesh and has broken repeatedly since 2023. Even if it were licensed, the mesh is unfit for site design: buildings, trees and ground are one fused skin with no published accuracy specification, and Google Earth elevations carry roughly **2.3 m RMSE against ~0.5 m for USGS lidar**. The legal path is also the more accurate and cheaper one: a free Census geocode, the county's parcel polygon, **USGS 3DEP lidar (public domain, ≥2 pts/m², 10 cm vertical RMSE)**, Overture or Microsoft building footprints and NAIP or county orthoimagery, converted with free PDAL/GDAL/QGIS and brought into Civil 3D via ReCap, or pre-packaged by Equator for $169/month. The data costs nothing; the software is the real bill (**Civil 3D ≈ $2,870/yr, AEC Collection ≈ $3,400–3,800/yr**, or BricsCAD Pro and SketchUp Pro for far less); and a **boundary-plus-topographic survey (roughly $1,000–3,000)** must replace the GIS parcel line before anything is submitted for a permit.

## Google's contracts, not its servers, are the wall

The product you would be extracting is the Map Tiles API's "Photorealistic 3D Tiles": the same mesh Google Earth renders, streamed as OGC 3D Tiles whose payloads are Draco-compressed binary glTF, starting from `https://tile.googleapis.com/v1/3dtiles/root.json` with a session token good for at least three hours of tile requests ([deck.gl Tile3DLayer docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/visgl/deck.gl/master/docs/api-reference/geo-layers/tile-3d-layer.md); [3DTilesRendererJS issue #1758](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/issues/1758)). Google's Aerial View proto confirms the lineage: its videos "are built with the same 3D map source used by Google Earth" ([Aerial View API overview](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/aerial-view/overview), search snippet). Pulling those tiles is cheap and easy. Third-party compilations of Google's price list put the SKU at **1,000 free root-tileset requests per month, then $6.00 per 1,000**, with every child tile inside a session free ([rioharper/vantage research](https://github.com/rioharper/vantage/blob/main/docs/research/market-freemium-launch.md); [Poglavar/consensus-builder](https://github.com/Poglavar/consensus-builder/blob/main/realistic-3d.md)), and Blosm's wiki tells users they get "1000 imports of Google 3D Tiles for free" a month once a billing account exists ([Blosm wiki](https://github.com/vvoovv/blosm/wiki/Import-of-Google-3D-Cities)). Downloading a whole neighbourhood therefore costs pennies; the barrier is entirely contractual.

That contract is unambiguous. Section 3.2.3(a) of the Maps Platform Terms states that "Customer will not export, extract, or otherwise scrape Google Maps Content for use outside the Services," including "pre-fetch, index, store, reshare, or rehost" and "bulk download Google Maps tiles ... elevation values"; §3.2.3(b) forbids caching "except as expressly permitted under the Maps Service Specific Terms"; and §3.2.3(c) says "Customer will not create content based on Google Maps Content," with the examples "create 3D building models from 45° Imagery" and "build terrain models based on elevation values from the Elevation API" ([Google Maps Platform ToS](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)). The Service Specific Terms contain per-API caching allowances for Places, Routes, Solar and others but **no caching section at all for the Map Tiles API or the Elevation API**, so the default no-caching rule applies with no exception ([Service Specific Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)). On top of that, the Map Tiles API Policies page, which this research could not fetch directly but which is quoted verbatim and consistently by several independent implementers, prohibits "non-visualization use cases" including "geodata extraction" and "offline uses" and states that "you may not have 3D objects extracted, traced, or otherwise derived by hand or machine from Photorealistic 3D Tiles" ([Map Tiles API Policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies), via third-party quotations in [RoofGrid roof-engine.ts](https://github.com/CertifiedWebMaster/RoofGrid/blob/main/lib/roof-report/engine/roof-engine.ts) and [heat-canyon photoreal.js](https://github.com/faizanraza09/heat-canyon/blob/main/web/js/photoreal.js)). One roofing startup retired its 3D Tiles pipeline on July 2, 2026 for exactly this reason after a policy refresh dated June 29, 2026 ([RoofGrid](https://github.com/CertifiedWebMaster/RoofGrid/blob/main/lib/roof-report/engine/roof-engine.ts)). Importing GLB tiles into Blender and re-exporting to DWG is an export, a store and a derived 3D object all at once; even hand-tracing a house from the tiles is named conduct.

The consumer products are no looser. The new Google Earth Service Specific Terms (§8.2) say "Customer will not, and will not allow End Users to, download, export or copy Data layers and tables from Google Earth," and §8.3.2 adds that Google Earth's own design features "should not be used for construction or structural engineering designs" ([Service Specific Terms §8](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)). Google Earth Pro exports only what you draw (KML/KMZ), images and movies; no 3D-model export exists ([Bellingcat toolkit: Google Earth Pro](https://github.com/bellingcat/toolkit/blob/main/gitbook/tools/google-earth-pro/README.md)). Google's Earth Studio FAQ states that "Google currently does not offer a license to use Google Earth imagery for commercial applications" ([Earth Studio FAQ](https://www.google.com/earth/studio/faq/), search snippet). The only era in which building geometry was openly downloadable was the SketchUp/3D Warehouse pipeline, retired on October 1, 2013 ([3D Warehouse group announcement](https://groups.google.com/g/3dwh/c/epXUQA2bJ2s), search snippet).

Two Google products do hand over real height data as files, and neither is usable for this purpose. The Solar API's `dataLayers:get` returns a **0.1 m/pixel Digital Surface Model GeoTIFF** (heights above EGM96), an RGB ortho and a building mask for a radius up to 100 m, and `buildingInsights` returns per-roof-segment pitch, azimuth and plane height ([Solar API discovery document](https://solar.googleapis.com/$discovery/rest?version=v1)). But §20.1 limits the Solar API to solar-energy feasibility, design or a solar "Downstream Transaction," and §20.2 requires cached Solar Data to be deleted after **30 consecutive days** unless embedded in a solar proposal ([Service Specific Terms §20](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)); a general site model is not a downstream transaction, as an independent July 2026 legal review reached the same conclusion for non-solar uses ([quotemate painting-api-review](https://github.com/MaintainTechnology/quotemate/blob/main/quotemate-automation/docs/painting-api-review.md)). The Elevation API returns interpolated points whose "resolution" field grows coarser as you ask for more of them ([google-maps-services-js elevation.ts](https://github.com/googlemaps/google-maps-services-js/blob/master/src/elevation.ts)), and building a terrain model from it is the ToS's own example of prohibited conduct.

The one Google channel that legitimately yields exportable height data is Earth Engine, and only because the data is not Google's. Earth Engine hosts the **USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM** under USGS public-domain terms ("may be used without restriction") and its `image.export` writes GeoTIFFs to Drive ([earthengine-catalog USGS_3DEP_1m.jsonnet](https://github.com/google/earthengine-catalog/blob/main/catalog/USGS/USGS_3DEP_1m.jsonnet); [Earth Engine REST discovery](https://earthengine.googleapis.com/$discovery/rest?version=v1)). Commercial use needs a paid plan (the "Limited" plan is usage-fees only; "Basic" is $500/month), while nonprofit and research use stays free ([Earth Engine pricing](https://cloud.google.com/earth-engine/pricing)). For a single parcel, though, the same 1 m DEM is a free download from USGS directly, so Earth Engine is a convenience, not a necessity. On enforcement, the record is thin: the flight-sim community's working rule is that "personal use that stays on your PC is considered acceptable" while distributing is not ([FSDeveloper thread](https://www.fsdeveloper.com/forum/threads/can-you-sell-sceneries-if-you-use-render-doc-to-capture-google-earth-buildings.452039/), search snippet), and no lawsuit against an individual was found. But a site plan is meant to be shown to buyers, planners and contractors, which is the exact point at which the tool authors, the forums and the contract all say the exposure becomes real, and where a design professional adds negligence risk by relying on data with no accuracy specification.

## The videos extract terrain or hack the GPU; none yields editable buildings

The most-viewed "Google Earth to AutoCAD 3D terrain" videos are Plex-Earth product demos from 2016–2019 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjr1YN6t5V0)), and Plex-Earth's own support pages explain what they show: the plugin's default elevation "comes from Google Elevation, which is based on a combination of global datasets with a general horizontal resolution of 1–30 meters," and "Plex-Earth does not support direct import of 3D building models from Google Earth, as Google does not allow access to this data" ([Plex-Earth providers article](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/25594243823645-Useful-Information-about-our-Providers); [Plex-Earth Importing Terrain](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000685983-Importing-Terrain), both search snippets). The "3D" in those videos is a bare terrain mesh triangulated from web elevation samples inside the user's polygon, and the vendor itself labels it suited to "preliminary design, feasibility studies, and visualization" that "does not replace certified local surveys" ([Plex-Earth Terrain](https://plexearth.com/features/terrain), search snippet). Autodesk removed Civil 3D's native Google Earth surface import after the 2012 release "due to some legal issue" ([Civil 3D Plus](https://civil3dplus.wordpress.com/2012/04/30/alternative-to-importing-google-earth-in-civil-3d/), search snippet), which is precisely the gap Plex-Earth and CAD-Earth fill. Other popular titles ("Get Data From Google Earth to AutoCAD Civil 3D", "Google Earth Contours to AutoCAD-Revit-ArchiCAD") describe the same point-and-contour generation, and "how to export 3D model Building in Google Earth by autocad civil 3d" is about pushing CAD models into Google Earth as KML, not the reverse ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VeOhJIPSPZg); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2lSlqtpO5gw)).

The videos that do show Google's textured buildings landing in Blender use RenderDoc: it is injected into Chrome's GPU process while Google Maps or Earth renders in 3D, the draw calls are saved to an `.rdc` capture, and Elie Michel's MapsModelsImporter add-on rebuilds the meshes and textures ([MapsModelsImporter README](https://github.com/eliemichel/MapsModelsImporter)). The author calls it "a proof of concept ... intended for educational purpose only" and says "Do not use this for any commercial nor redistribution purpose"; the last release of the original project is **v0.7.0 of April 5, 2024**, locked to RenderDoc 1.31 and Blender 4.1 ([Releases](https://github.com/eliemichel/MapsModelsImporter/releases)). Its 2025–2026 issue tracker is a run of unanswered breakage reports: Chrome 137 broke the direct-composition workaround in May 2025 and Google Earth stopped rendering WebGL under injection in December 2025 ([issue #361](https://github.com/eliemichel/MapsModelsImporter/issues/361); [issue #365](https://github.com/eliemichel/MapsModelsImporter/issues/365)). A community fork, MapsModelsImporter-Enhanced v0.5.0 (April 2026), claims support for RenderDoc up to 1.43 and Blender 5.1, but its own README says commercial use is "strictly prohibited," that "large-scale captures risk account suspension," and that scale and positioning "depend on RenderDoc capture data" with no georeferencing ([Sheep-max/MapsModelsImporter-Enhanced](https://github.com/Sheep-max/MapsModelsImporter-Enhanced)). A user asking for even "one geolocated point" to place the model got no reply ([issue #355](https://github.com/eliemichel/MapsModelsImporter/issues/355)), and RenderDoc's maintainer bans discussion of capturing "Google Maps or Google Earth" in every official channel ([baldurk/renderdoc](https://github.com/baldurk/renderdoc)). The output is a screen-sized pile of per-draw-call mesh chunks (default cap 200 blocks), in arbitrary units, at Blender's origin, which is the worst possible starting point for placing homes on a real parcel.

The API-key route is technically better and legally worse. Blosm downloads the tiles for an extent you draw, assembles them in metres with a stored `_lat_`/`_lon_` origin in a local Transverse Mercator frame, and the free base version suffices for an OBJ export ([Blosm Georeferenced Scene wiki](https://github.com/vvoovv/blosm/wiki/Georeferenced-Blender-Scene); [chenyingshu/google_3dtile_collection](https://github.com/chenyingshu/google_3dtile_collection)). But the wiki states outright that **"Buildings are not separate objects,"** that imported materials "cannot be directly exported" until replaced, and that a 2.3 × 1.5 km piece of Amsterdam at full detail "yielded over 2.5 million triangles across 500+ tiles" ([Import of 3D Tiles wiki](https://github.com/vvoovv/blosm/wiki/Import-of-3D-Tiles); [Import of Google 3D Cities wiki](https://github.com/vvoovv/blosm/wiki/Import-of-Google-3D-Cities)). Smaller scripts (3dtiles-dl, nodejs-3dtiles-downloader, google-earth-as-gltf) do the same without Blender, re-centring from Earth-centred ECEF coordinates; one of them prints "Offline use is prohibited" in its README ([google-earth-as-gltf](https://github.com/OmarShehata/google-earth-as-gltf); [nodejs-3dtiles-downloader](https://github.com/voxelearth/nodejs-3dtiles-downloader)). Heron for Rhino/Grasshopper added the same download components in v0.5.0 ([Heron releases](https://github.com/blueherongis/Heron/releases)). A third, older technique scrapes Google Earth's internal "rocktree" octants without any key; its reference implementation was archived in August 2022, and the flight-sim "Google Earth Decoder" built on it broke around July 2025 when Google changed the imagery epoch and was binary-patched back to life in 2026 ([retroplasma/earth-reverse-engineering](https://github.com/retroplasma/earth-reverse-engineering); [flightsim.to "Fixed 2026"](https://flightsim.to/addon/112579/google-earth-decoder-earth2msfs-fixed-2026), search snippet). Screenshot photogrammetry, feeding many Earth screen grabs to structure-from-motion software, reproduces Google's mesh to about 1% planimetric and 5% vertical error relative to Google itself, not to the ground ([Chen & Clarke 2016](https://www.cartogis.org/docs/proceedings/2016/Chen_and_Clarke.pdf), search snippet).

Suppose you ignore the terms. What you hold is still not a site model. Google publishes no accuracy specification for the mesh; Cesium users report Everest reading about 8,700 m against 8,849 m and models that "sink into the earth" without height correction ([Cesium Community](https://community.cesium.com/t/how-accurate-are-google-photorealistic-3d-tiles-altitudes/30155), search snippet). A peer-reviewed study of Google Earth's imagery archive found **39.7 m RMSE horizontal positional error** overall ([PMC3791001](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3791001/)), and another found Google Earth roadway elevation errors of **1.32 m MAE and 2.27 m RMSE**, while Google's own documentation says its measurements "are estimates, may not be 100% accurate, and are not substitutes for onsite or survey-grade measurements" ([ETASR study](https://www.etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/7121); [Google Earth measure docs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/earth/measure-distances?authuser=7), both search snippets). The surface is one continuous skin: walls flare into the ground, overhangs are filled, trees are blobs, and nothing in it marks a parcel line, easement, setback or finished-floor elevation. Then comes the last mile. AutoCAD's IMPORT command reads STEP, IGES, Parasolid, Rhino 3DM and the major MCAD formats but has no native OBJ, glTF or FBX import; FBXIMPORT was removed in AutoCAD 2019 ([Autodesk: About Importing Non-Autodesk 3D Models](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/AutoCAD-Core/files/GUID-2CF5CE0F-8049-459E-86C9-1826B41957F6.htm); [Autodesk KB: FBX removed](https://knowledge.autodesk.com/support/autocad/troubleshooting/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/fbximport-and-fbxexport-removed-from-autocad.html)). Blender's DXF exporter has been an unbundled "limited support" extension since 4.2 ([Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/export-autocad-dxf-format-dxf/), search snippet), and the free Automesher LT add-on can read OBJ/STL into AutoCAD ([Autodesk App Store: Automesher LT](https://apps.autodesk.com/ACD/en/Detail/Index?id=6592083963201800613&appLang=en&os=Win32_64)). Every chain ends the same way: an untextured polyface mesh in a DWG that cannot be snapped to reliably and is not a Civil 3D surface. No documented case of a Google mesh becoming a Civil 3D grading surface was found.

## From address to Civil 3D in seven steps

The workflow below is the one the evidence supports, and every input in it is either public domain or ODbL-licensed with an attribution notice. It is written for a single US parcel of roughly 0.1–5 acres; the ordering is an inference from the documented mechanics of each tool.

**Step 1, geocode the address for free.** The Census Bureau's `onelineaddress` endpoint takes a single address string and returns longitude/latitude as JSON, with a batch CSV option ([Census Geocoding Services API](https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/Geocoding_Services_API.pdf)); the `censusgeocode` Python package wraps it ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/censusgeocode/)).

**Step 2, pull the parcel polygon and treat it as reference only.** Most counties expose an ArcGIS REST "parcels" FeatureServer; a point-in-polygon query with `f=geojson` returns the lot outline and its attributes ([Esri: Query Feature Service](https://developers.arcgis.com/rest/services-reference/enterprise/query-feature-service-layer/)). Where the county has none, Regrid's self-serve API includes 2,000 parcel records a month, or a whole county with premium fields costs **$400** as a shapefile ([Regrid changelog](https://support.regrid.com/changelog/self-serve-api-plans); [Regrid Pay-to-Download](https://regrid.com/pay-to-download-parcel-data)). Put that polygon on its own reference layer: "it is normal for a GIS line to be off by five, ten, or even twenty feet," and county portals disclaim their lines for legal use ([Godfrey Hoffman Hodge](https://ghhllc.com/blog/understanding-town-gis-portals-what-those-lines-really-mean); [Tuolumne County GIS disclaimer](https://tuolumnecounty.ca.gov/524/GIS-Layer-Descriptions)). The recorded plat's bearings and distances, or a boundary survey, replace it before setbacks are drawn.

**Step 3, clip USGS 3DEP lidar and a 1 m DEM for the lot.** By the end of FY2025, **99% of the nation** had 3DEP-quality lidar available or in progress; the minimum quality level, QL2, is **≥2 points/m² with 10 cm RMSEz**, and the data is US Government public domain ([USGS: What is 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/what-3dep); [USGS quality levels](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/topographic-data-quality-levels-qls), both search snippets; [AWS Open Data registry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/usgs-lidar.yaml)). The fastest route is the public Entwine bucket: a PDAL pipeline with `readers.ept` pointed at `https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/usgs-lidar-public/<Project>/ept.json` and a `bounds` clip in EPSG:3857 streams only your lot's points ([PDAL readers.ept](https://pdal.io/en/stable/stages/readers.ept.html), search snippet; [hobuinc/usgs-lidar](https://github.com/hobuinc/usgs-lidar)); OpenTopography's notebooks show the same clip producing DTM, DSM and canopy rasters ([OT_3DEP_Workflows](https://github.com/OpenTopography/OT_3DEP_Workflows)). Filter class 2 for ground and class 6 for building roofs ([LidarVisor classification codes](https://lidarvisor.com/lidar-classification-codes/)). If you prefer files, TNMAccess lists LAZ tiles and the 1 m DEM for a bounding box ([TNMAccess API](https://tnmaccess.nationalmap.gov/api/v1/docs)); note that OpenTopography restricts the 1 m DEM to academic users, so non-academics go to TNM or AWS ([OpenTopography API docs](https://portal.opentopography.org/apidocs/), search snippet). The 1 m DEM is bare earth (buildings removed); build the DSM yourself from first returns if you want the existing house's ridge height. Contours come from `gdal_contour -3d -i <interval>` written straight to DXF or to a shapefile for Civil 3D ([GDAL gdal_contour](https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/blob/master/doc/source/programs/gdal_contour.rst)). The seamless service including 1 m DEMs has a stated **0.53 m RMSE**, versus the 2.27 m Google Earth figure above ([USGS FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-vertical-accuracy-seamless-3dep-dems), search snippet).

**Step 4, add footprints and imagery.** Overture's CLI returns buildings for a bounding box in one command (`overturemaps download --bbox=... --type=building`), with `height`, `num_floors` and `roof_shape` where known; some US heights are derived from 3DEP lidar ([overturemaps-py](https://github.com/OvertureMaps/overturemaps-py); [Overture Buildings Guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/buildings/)). Microsoft's US footprints (129.6 million polygons, ODbL, per-state GeoJSON) and OSM via Overpass are the alternatives; Google Open Buildings does not cover the US ([microsoft/USBuildingFootprints](https://github.com/microsoft/USBuildingFootprints); [Google Open Buildings](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/)). All three are ODbL, so a distributed DWG needs an OpenStreetMap attribution note ([OSM Foundation attribution guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines)). For imagery, NAIP is public domain at 0.3–1 m and served as COGs ([Planetary Computer NAIP](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/naip), search snippet; [data.gov NAIP](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/national-agriculture-imagery-program-naip-imagery)), and many states publish free 6-inch orthos (New York county downloads, Indiana 2023 statewide) ([NYS orthoimagery](https://gis.ny.gov/orthoimagery-download-county); [Indiana Spatial Data Portal](https://gis.iu.edu/s/isdp/item-set/71428)). Coastal parcels should check NOAA's Data Access Viewer, which clips lidar to a drawn area in your choice of format, projection and datum ([NOAA DAV](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/tools/dav.html)).

**Step 5, set the coordinate system before importing anything.** Assign the NAD83(2011) State Plane zone in Civil 3D's Drawing Settings (not GEOGRAPHICLOCATION, which misplaces Bing imagery), using the foot definition your state and the lidar metadata use; NAD83 State Plane is metric-based and states differ on US survey foot (1200/3937 m) versus international foot (0.3048 m) ([IMAGINiT: International vs Survey Foot](https://resources.imaginit.com/civil-solutions-blog/september-survey-series-part-1-international-foot-vs-survey-foot); [Autodesk: Bing map wrong location](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Bing-Maps-appear-in-the-wrong-location-in-Civil-3D.html)). Lidar deliveries can mix horizontal international feet with vertical US survey feet ([rpls.com](https://rpls.com/forums/ask-a-surveyor/civil-engineer-importance-and-effect-of-survey-data-coordinate-systems-and-civil-3d-topo/)); record NAVD88 and the geoid in the title block (NAVD88 in US survey feet is EPSG:6360 ([epsg.io](https://epsg.io/6360))). Reproject TNM's geographic-coordinate DEMs with `gdalwarp` first, because Autodesk warns that creating a surface from USGS GeoTIFFs "may result in an incorrect geodetic transformation" ([Autodesk KB](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Creating-surfaces-from-USGS-GeoTIFF-DEM-files.html)).

**Step 6, build the existing-ground surface in Civil 3D.** Plain AutoCAD accepts only RCS/RCP point clouds, so the LAZ goes through ReCap Pro, where you set the source and target coordinate systems and delete non-ground points ([Autodesk: point cloud formats](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Which-point-cloud-file-formats-can-AutoCAD-import.html); [Autodesk: ground surface from Lidar](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/How-to-create-ground-surface-from-Lidar-in-Civil-3D.html)). In Civil 3D, attach the RCP, restrict the point-cloud style to class "2 Ground," and run Create Surface from Point Cloud with a distance-between-points of a few feet for a lot-scale site ([Autodesk: surface with only ground points](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/How-to-create-a-Surface-with-Only-Ground-Points-from-Point-Cloud-Data-in-Civil-3D.html); [Autodesk Community](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/civil-3d-forum/attach-point-cloud-and-convert-to-surface/td-p/10211481)). The fallback is adding the 1 m GeoTIFF directly to a TIN surface's DEM Files definition ([draftsperson.net](https://blog.draftsperson.net/import-a-lidar-tif-file-into-civil-3d/)). Parcel and footprint shapefiles come in through the Import GIS Data wizard (SHP, SDF, GML, MIF/MID, TAB, with automatic transformation); convert GeoJSON to SHP in QGIS first, since GeoJSON support was not confirmed ([Autodesk Help: Import GIS Data Wizard](https://help.autodesk.com/view/CIV3D/2025/ENU/?guid=GUID-06592A03-796B-4CCE-A115-6B521375CA36)). Bing aerials via GEOMAP are fine as context but carry Microsoft copyright obligations when shared ([Autodesk: Bing copyright implications](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Copyright-implications-from-sharing-Infraworks-or-Civil-3D-models-that-use-Bing-Maps-imagery.html)); the NAIP or county ortho from Step 4 carries none.

**Step 7, design the layout, then survey before you permit.** Civil 3D's parcel tools (Slide Line, Swing Line, Freeform) subdivide a site by minimum area, frontage, width and depth ([Noble Desktop: parcel creation](https://blog.nobledesktop.com/learn/civil-3d/automatic-subdivision-parcel-creation-methods-in-civil-3d)); the residential-projects course defines each building pad as a polyline snapped to lot corners, offset inside the setback, converted to a feature line ([LinkedIn Learning: Define the building pad](https://www.linkedin.com/learning/autocad-civil-3d-designing-residential-projects/define-the-building-pad)); a private drive is a short corridor (alignment, profile, assembly) so it produces daylight lines and a finished-grade surface ([Noble Desktop: corridors](https://blog.nobledesktop.com/learn/civil-3d/creating-corridors-with-assemblies-in-civil-3d-step-by-step-guide)); and Grading Optimization, an AEC Collection entitlement, balances pads against slope, elevation and drainage constraints ([AUGI: Grading Optimization](https://www.augi.com/articles/detail/civil-3d-grading-optimization)). Model each tiny-home type once as a DWG and place it as a block or Xref on its pad, keeping existing ground, design surfaces, site plan and units in separate drawings tied by data shortcuts. Before submission, commission a boundary plus topographic survey: 2026 guides put boundary surveys at roughly **$380–1,200** (complex lots to ~$5,500), topographic at **$400–1,500** and ALTA at **$1,200–4,000+**, and municipalities require a surveyed site plan for a building permit ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/land-survey-cost); [ALTA Survey Colorado](https://altasurveycolorado.com/average-cost-for-a-land-survey-2026-price-guide/); [Exacta Land Surveyors](https://exactaland.com/survey-types/)). The surveyor's DWG then becomes the authoritative base to which the open-data layers are re-aligned.

## Alternatives trade dollars for accuracy and convenience

The software line dominates the budget. Autodesk FAQ figures as surfaced on September 28, 2026 put AutoCAD at **$2,095/year**, Civil 3D at **$2,870/year** and the AEC Collection, which bundles both with ReCap Pro, InfraWorks, Revit, Forma and Grading Optimization, at about **$3,375–3,795/year** depending on the aggregator ([AutoCAD Tips](https://autocadtips.com/blog/autocad-price-explained-monthly-and-annual-plans/); [Autodesk Civil 3D FAQ](https://www.autodesk.com/solutions/aec/civil-3d-subscription-faq), search snippet; [BIM Tools Hub](https://bimtoolshub.com/autodesk-forma-site-design); [AutodeskAudits](https://autodesksaudits.com/blog/autodesk-aec-collection-guide/)). AutoCAD alone cannot finish the job: it has no tool to build a TIN from points, contours or DEMs ("the only AutoCAD workflow is to use Civil 3D"), though the bundled Map 3D toolset does assign coordinate systems and import shapefiles at no extra cost ([Autodesk Community: Create Surface from Points](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/autocad-map-3d-forum/create-surface-from-points/td-p/9985470); [Graitec: Map 3D toolset included](https://graitec.com/us/tech-resources/map-3d-toolset-is-included-with-autocad/)). ReCap Pro standalone is roughly **$305–370/year** ([SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/3d-design-software/autodesk-recap/); [Novedge](https://novedge.com/products/buy-recap-pro-subscription)). If you already pay for AutoCAD, switching the renewal to the AEC Collection costs less than adding Civil 3D separately, and a single month of Civil 3D (about $360) is a defensible option for a one-off design. Cheaper DWG-native routes exist: BricsCAD Pro sells a perpetual licence at "about half of a yearly AutoCAD subscription" and the free LidarTools add-on goes from LAS/LAZ to a TIN surface ([Bricsys: License types](https://help.bricsys.com/en-us/document/bricscad/installation-and-licensing/overview-of-licensing-options/license-types); [Bricsys forum](https://forum.bricsys.com/discussion/36568/creating-a-tin-surface-from-point-cloud-workflow-and-optimisations)), and SketchUp Pro (**$399/year**) plus PlaceMaker (**$109/year**) gives terrain, aerials and massing for roughly 200 million US building footprints, exporting DWG back to AutoCAD ([Vagon: SketchUp pricing](https://vagon.io/blog/sketchup-pricing); [PlaceMaker](https://www.suplacemaker.com/placemaker-shop-now/), search snippet).

Equator is the most direct paid substitute for the free pipeline: it clips USGS lidar, generates contours and exports "DWG, DXF, or GeoJSON — pre-formatted for Civil 3D and AutoCAD" plus LAZ point clouds, for **$169/month** with a free first contour map ([Equator: Contours and LiDAR](https://equatorstudios.com/contours-and-lidar/); [Equator pricing](https://equatorstudios.com/pricing/), both search snippets). Because the accuracy is identical to the DIY route, the $169 buys hours, not precision; one month per project is the rational purchase. Plex-Earth makes sense only if you want a single in-AutoCAD workflow, and then you should import the USGS 1 m GeoTIFF it supports as a "custom terrain source" rather than rely on the default Google Elevation service ([Plex-Earth: USGS 1 m DEM](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/27757192210717-Terrain-Data-GeoTIFF-USGS-1-meter-resolution-DEM), search snippet); its advertised price is either $499 or $590 a year depending on the listing ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/plex-earth/reviews), search snippet; [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/)). Cadmapper's free DWG is useful for neighbouring roads and footprints but its **4 m minimum contour interval** on 30 m SRTM makes its terrain decorative for a residential lot ([Rascoh Studio](https://rascoh.com/3d-site-plan-cadmapper-autocad-tutorial/), search snippet). InfraWorks Model Builder and Forma are Autodesk's own address-to-context generators, but Model Builder's US terrain is 10 m/30 m NED and Autodesk itself says its elevation accuracy "can vary substantially," so both are massing and visualization layers on top of your lidar surface, not replacements for it ([Autodesk: Source of terrain data in InfraWorks](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Source-of-Terrain-Data-in-Infraworks.html); [Autodesk: Model Builder accuracy](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/What-is-the-terrain-elevation-accuracy-for-Model-Builder-in-Infraworks.html), both search snippets).

If you want a textured 3D model of the existing house that you may legally keep, the choices are commercial data or your own camera. Nearmap is the one vendor with documented self-serve export of DSM, point cloud and textured mesh in OBJ, FBX, LAS and GeoTIFF for subscribers holding 3D-export permission and credits, with DSM accuracy it describes as "down to 10–30 cm or better" ([Nearmap: 3D export formats](https://help.nearmap.com/kb/articles/740-3d-export-formats); [Nearmap: Digital Surface Models](https://www.nearmap.com/topics-glossary/digital-surface-models), both search snippets); Maxar/Vantor's Precision3D at 50 cm resolution and 3 m SE90 is too coarse for a lot ([Esri partner page](https://www.esri.com/partners/maxar-a2T70000000TNOvEAO/precision3d-a2d5x000005kI0FAAU)). Flying your own drone is the more accurate option, but mapping your lot to plan a development is "in furtherance of a business" and therefore not recreational under FAA rules, so it needs a Part 107 certificate (**$175 test**, $5 registration) ([FAA FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/faq/how-recreational-flying-defined), search snippet; [Rotate: Part 107 cost](https://rotatepilot.com/guides/part-107-test-cost-and-scheduling)). WebODM is free under the AGPL and RealityScan (formerly RealityCapture) is free for companies under $1 million revenue ([OpenDroneMap/WebODM](https://github.com/OpenDroneMap/WebODM); [RealityScan licensing](https://www.realityscan.com/license)); with RTK or surveyed ground control points, expect **1–3 cm horizontal and 2–5 cm vertical** accuracy, whereas without them the model is internally consistent but mislocated by consumer-GNSS error ([McKissock: drone mapping accuracy](https://www.mckissock.com/blog/land-surveyor/drone-mapping-accuracy-gcps-rtk-ppk-and-explaining-tolerances/)). A DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise runs about **$3,899**, or $4,729 with the RTK module ([E38 Survey Solutions](https://e38surveysolutions.com/blogs/news/dji-mavic-3-enterprise-price)). The resulting LAS goes through ReCap Pro to Civil 3D exactly like the USGS data, and the OBJ mesh stays in a visualization tool.

## Which claims rest on snippets rather than primary pages

The research sandbox could not open developers.google.com, support.google.com, mapsplatform.google.com, most vendor sites (plexearth.com, cad-earth.com, equatorstudios.com, suplacemaker.com, nearmap.com), autodesk.com and help.autodesk.com, usgs.gov, opentopography.org, faa.gov, reddit or the SketchUp and Cesium forums. The strongest evidence in this report is therefore the legal text: the Maps Platform Terms of Service, the Service Specific Terms (including the Google Earth §8 and Solar API §20 clauses), the Core Services Summary and the Earth Engine pricing page were all fetched from cloud.google.com and quoted verbatim, as were the Solar API and Earth Engine discovery documents and every GitHub-hosted tool README, wiki, issue and release page cited above. Weaker, snippet-only evidence includes: the Map Tiles API Policies wording ("extracted, traced, or otherwise derived by hand or machine," "offline uses"), which is second-hand but reproduced identically by at least four independent implementers and by a July 2026 compliance decision; the Photorealistic 3D Tiles price table ($6.00 per 1,000 root requests, 1,000 free), which comes only from third-party compilations of Google's list; the Solar API price figures; every Autodesk list price (AutoCAD, Civil 3D, Revit, AEC Collection, ReCap, Forma), which conflict by 5–15% across aggregators and should be re-read on autodesk.com before being quoted; the Plex-Earth, CAD-Earth, Equator, Cadmapper, PlaceMaker and Nearmap product claims and prices; the USGS 3DEP coverage, quality-level and RMSE figures (the QL2 specification and public-domain licence are corroborated by fetched GitHub mirrors, but the 99% coverage and 0.53 m RMSE numbers are snippet-sourced); the Google Earth roadway RMSE study and the Everest altitude anecdote; and the FAA recreational-versus-business distinction. The Google Maps/Google Earth consumer Additional Terms, which govern RenderDoc and screenshot use of earth.google.com, could not be opened at all and are not quoted here. No quantitative assessment of Google's 3D mesh against survey control was found anywhere; the correct statement is that Google publishes no accuracy specification, not that a particular number applies.

## Conclusion

The question "how do I get the Google Earth model into AutoCAD" turns out to be a category error rather than a technical challenge. Google Earth's mesh is a rendering product licensed for looking at, and every 2025–2026 change found (per-SKU billing, the EEA cut-off, the June 2026 policy refresh, Esri and ArcGIS streaming, the new Google Earth terms) either tightened extraction or widened viewing; none created an export licence. The people who appear to succeed in videos are either buying a terrain-only plugin whose vendor concedes it cannot touch Google's buildings, or running a GPU hack whose own authors call it non-commercial and un-georeferenced. Meanwhile the thing a site designer actually needs, a bare-earth surface with a known datum, crisp parcel edges and building footprints with attributes, is exactly what the Google mesh cannot provide and what the US public-data ecosystem gives away: the federal government has already flown lidar over 99% of the country at a quality level Google does not claim to match, and Google's only legitimate role in that pipeline is as one of several free mirrors of USGS data through Earth Engine.

The practical implication is to reallocate the budget. Spend nothing on Google, a few hours on PDAL/GDAL/QGIS (or $169 for a month of Equator), and put the money into the CAD seat that can turn a point cloud into a graded site, which for an existing AutoCAD subscriber means renewing into the AEC Collection rather than buying Civil 3D on the side, or dropping to BricsCAD Pro or SketchUp Pro plus PlaceMaker if engineering-grade grading is not needed. Treat everything downloaded as a pre-design model whose job is to test how many homes and tiny homes fit and roughly how the drive and pads will grade; the first dollars spent on the property itself should go to a boundary-and-topo survey, because that DWG, not any download, is what a permit office, a lender and a title company will accept.

## Applied example: the Sausage Castle, Astatula FL

The workflow above was run in this session for 22500 Robbins Road, Astatula, FL 34705 using only public data, and the results are in `reports/sausage-castle-base/`: a layered DXF in NAD83 Florida East US survey feet with 1 ft contours, 68 building footprints extruded to lidar-measured heights, roads, the two lakes and address points; a 317,000-point bare-earth PNEZD file for Civil 3D; a 3 ft DTM raster; a hillshade preview; and the three Python scripts that produced them from the 2018 USGS Lake County lidar (39.5 million points) and Overture Maps release 2026-09-23.1. The address was verified against Overture address points and road geometry after a search-engine coordinate proved to be 1.7 km off.
