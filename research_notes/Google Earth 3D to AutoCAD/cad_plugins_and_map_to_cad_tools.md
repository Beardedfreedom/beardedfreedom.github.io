# Commercial CAD plugins and "map-to-CAD" tools that claim to bring Google Earth / online map data into AutoCAD, Civil 3D, SketchUp, Rhino, Revit — what each actually delivers for one US parcel

Research date: 2026-09-28. Prices are as seen on that date unless noted. **Method caveat:** the research sandbox's egress proxy blocked direct fetches of most vendor sites (plexearth.com, support.plexearth.com, cad-earth.com, spatialmanager.com, cadmapper.com, equatorstudios.com, suplacemaker.com, placemakerdata.com, help.sketchup.com, forums.sketchup.com, forums.autodesk.com, apps.autodesk.com, help.autodesk.com, reddit) and the session's web-search budget was exhausted mid-task. Most vendor facts below therefore come from search-engine snippets that quote the linked vendor page (marked "(via search snippet)"), plus a handful of pages that were fully fetched (SourceForge, GitHub repos/wikis, the Heron release page). Report writer should treat snippet-sourced prices as "as advertised, not independently verified on the page."

## Key Question 1: Plex-Earth (Plexscape) — features in 2026, data sources, outputs, pricing, supported products, licensing

### Takeaway
Plex-Earth is the most-cited "Google Earth to AutoCAD" plugin in tutorials, but its 2026 product is really a multi-provider imagery + terrain importer for AutoCAD/Civil 3D/BricsCAD: imagery comes from Google Maps/Azure(Bing)/OSM basemaps and paid premium aerials (Nearmap, Vexcel, Hexagon, Airbus, Vantor/Maxar); terrain defaults to the Google Elevation service (1–30 m effective resolution) with optional import of public DEMs such as USGS 1 m GeoTIFF; it produces contours, meshes, points or a Civil 3D surface — and it explicitly cannot import Google Earth's 3D buildings because Google does not allow access to that data. Advertised entry price is $499–$590/year depending on source (conflict noted below).

### Cited Findings
- Vendor positioning: "Plex-Earth brings design-ready imagery, terrain, and Google Earth tools into AutoCAD® and Civil 3D®, powered by Airbus, Maxar, Nearmap, and more." — [Plex-Earth home (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/)
- Licensing model: monthly, annual, or perpetual plans; standalone or floating licenses; cloud-based access, no license server; subscription includes first-year access to historical datasets up to 10 years old ("Timeviews") and version updates. — [Plex-Earth pricing page (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/pricing)
- Pricing as listed by SourceForge on 2026-09-28: "Starting Price: $590 per year"; monthly $75; perpetual license $1,475; free trial available. — [SourceForge product listing](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/)
- Pricing per search-engine summary of the vendor/G2 pages the same day: "Pricing starts at $499 per year." — [G2 Plex-Earth reviews (via search snippet)](https://www.g2.com/products/plex-earth/reviews); contradicted by [SourceForge ($590/yr)](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/). The named plan tiers (e.g., Standard/Professional/Premium) and what differentiates them could not be retrieved.
- Trial: "With a free trial you get access to all features of Plex-Earth 2026 in the Professional plan" — 7-day full access. — [Plex-Earth free trial (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/free-trial)
- Supported products: "compatible with AutoCAD 2020–2027, Civil 3D, Map 3D & BricsCAD." — [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/). The Autodesk App Store already lists a "Plex-Earth 2027" build for AutoCAD. — [Autodesk App Store listing](https://apps.autodesk.com/ACD/en/Detail/Index?id=5694471876005445115)
- Imagery sources: basemaps from Google Maps, Azure (Microsoft/Bing) and OpenStreetMap; premium imagery from Nearmap, Vexcel, Hexagon, Airbus and Vantor (the former Maxar); terrain from GeoTIFFs and DTM/DSM sources; described as a cloud SaaS tool connecting AutoCAD to mapping services. — [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/)
- Default terrain source: "Plex-Earth's default elevation data comes from Google Elevation, which is based on a combination of global datasets with a general horizontal resolution of 1–30 meters. Accuracy and recency vary by region." — [Plex-Earth Support: Useful Information about our Providers (via search snippet)](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/25594243823645-Useful-Information-about-our-Providers)
- US high-resolution terrain path: Plex-Earth publishes a support article "USA: 1 meter resolution DEM – USGS" under the section "Custom Terrain Data Sources (GeoTIFF & Elevation Datasets)", i.e., you bring in a USGS 1 m GeoTIFF rather than getting lidar-grade terrain from the default web service. — [Plex-Earth Support: USA 1 m DEM (via search snippet)](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/27757192210717-Terrain-Data-GeoTIFF-USGS-1-meter-resolution-DEM); section index: [Custom Terrain Data Sources](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/sections/32121394859549-Custom-Terrain-Data-Sources-GeoTIFF-Elevation-Datasets)
- Premium terrain: high-resolution DSM/DTM from Vantor, Nearmap and Vexcel "can be imported into Plex-Earth, but unlike imagery, premium terrain data is not available directly through the standard workflow—it must be acquired as a custom order." — [Plex-Earth Support: providers (via search snippet)](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/25594243823645-Useful-Information-about-our-Providers)
- Terrain outputs: "import real-world 3D terrain (elevation/topography) into your drawing using Plex-Earth by defining your area and generating terrain as contours, mesh, points, or a Civil 3D surface in AutoCAD/Civil 3D/BricsCAD." — [Plex-Earth Support: Create Terrain (via search snippet)](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/20197447070877-Create-Terrain); related how-tos: [Convert Plex-Earth's Surface to Civil 3D Surface](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409223637521-Convert-Plex-Earth-s-Surface-to-Civil-3D-Surface), [Create real-life Civil 3D Surface](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/5384574506001-Create-real-life-Civil-3D-Surface), [Creating a section in Civil 3D with Plex-Earth's surface](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404465197841-Creating-a-section-in-Civil-3D-with-Plex-Earth-s-surface)
- No Google 3D buildings: "Plex-Earth does not support direct import of 3D building models from Google Earth, as Google does not allow access to this data. Instead, the terrain tools import elevation surface data only." — [Plex-Earth Support: Importing Terrain (via search snippet)](https://support.plexearth.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000685983-Importing-Terrain)
- Google Earth objects: Plex-Earth's 2026 tutorial for "the easiest way to import objects from Google Earth into AutoCAD" is KML/KMZ copy-paste (i.e., vector placemarks/paths/polygons you draw in Google Earth), not building models. — [Plex-Earth tutorial (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/tutorials-import-kml-copy-paste)
- Accuracy disclaimer: accuracy "depends on the original source, location, resolution, and capture date, and is well-suited for preliminary design, feasibility studies, and visualization, but does not replace certified local surveys or as-built measurements for work with strict accuracy requirements." — [Plex-Earth Terrain feature page (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/features/terrain); also "Web elevation and public DEMs are usually excellent for planning, feasibility, and preliminary design, while detailed survey data will give you a higher-precision model." — [Plex-Earth Terrain (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/terrain)
- Reviews: SourceForge shows zero reviews ("This software hasn't been reviewed yet"). — [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Plex-Earth/). Vendor testimonials include "simple, effective, worth the cost and keeps getting better" and "saved us thousands of dollars and manhours." — [Plex-Earth testimonials (via search snippet)](https://plexearth.com/testimonials)
- YouTube: several of the top "Google Earth to AutoCAD 3D terrain" videos are Plex.Earth demos — "How to create a 3D Terrain with Google Earth in AutoCAD – Plex.Earth" (Apr 2019), "Plex.Earth 4: Import 3D terrain and Google Earth imagery into AutoCAD" (Jul 2017), "Importing Terrain from Google Earth to AutoCAD – Terrain from Current View" (Jan 2016). — [YouTube (search result listing)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjr1YN6t5V0), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-R3X1k5nHts), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BqUNMRWn9sA)
- Related product: Plexscape also ships "Plex-Earth for Autodesk Forma" with its own premium-providers FAQ. — [forma.plexearth.com FAQ (search result)](https://forma.plexearth.com/premium-providers-faq)

### Inferences
- What the YouTube "Google Earth → AutoCAD 3D terrain in 1 minute" videos actually show is Plex-Earth calling the Google Elevation web service on a grid inside the user's polygon and triangulating a mesh/contours; the "3D" is a bare terrain surface, never Google's photogrammetry buildings.
- For a US parcel, the default terrain is roughly 10 m-class (Google blends SRTM/NED-type data), so contours will be smooth and can be off by meters vertically; the meaningful upgrade is importing the USGS 3DEP 1 m GeoTIFF for the parcel, which Plex-Earth supports as a "custom terrain source."
- The $499 vs $590 discrepancy most likely reflects either a plan-tier difference or a 2025→2026 price change; treat "$500–$600/yr" as the working figure until verified on plexearth.com/pricing.

### Gaps
- Exact 2026 plan names/tiers and per-tier feature matrix (e.g., which tier includes Civil 3D surface creation, premium imagery, Timeviews) — pricing page blocked.
- Whether Plex-Earth 2026/2027 added any OSM-based building footprint/extrusion feature — not found; "no Google 3D buildings" is confirmed but OSM buildings status unknown.
- Independent user reviews (Reddit/CADTutor) on terrain accuracy — not retrievable in this session.

## Key Question 2: CAD-Earth (Arqcom) — features, data source, pricing, current status

### Takeaway
CAD-Earth is the lower-cost competitor ($149/yr Basic; $249/yr Plus) that imports satellite images and builds terrain meshes/contours/profiles inside AutoCAD 2018–2026 by sampling point elevations either from the Google Earth application or from a Cesium terrain server; it is Windows-only, still actively listed for AutoCAD 2026, and offers a 30-day trial. Like Plex-Earth it delivers imagery + terrain, not 3D buildings.

### Cited Findings
- Product description: "designed to easily import/export images, objects and terrain meshes between Google Earth and major CAD programs and create dynamic contour lines and profiles"; integrates with Google Earth and Cesium. — [Autodesk App Store: CAD-Earth (via search snippet)](https://apps.autodesk.com/acd/en/Detail/HelpDoc?appId=9157380413877632903&appLang=en&os=Win64)
- Terrain method: "The Plus version allows you to get terrain meshes, contour lines, profiles, and sections from Google Earth, with a terrain configuration mesh that can be created inside an existing closed polyline or along a polyline path, calculating point elevations from Google Earth with adjustable mesh density and precision." — [Autodesk App Store: CAD-Earth (via search snippet)](https://apps.autodesk.com/ACD/en/Detail/Index?id=9157380413877632903&appLang=en&os=Win64)
- Alternate elevation provider: "CAD-Earth has the option to select the Cesium terrain provider (www.cesium.com) to calculate terrain point elevations. Users can get point elevations and images directly from Google Earth instead of Cesium Terrain Server and the Google Image Server." — [CAD-Earth / Autodesk App Store (via search snippet)](https://cad-earth.com/)
- Other features: AI neural-network image enhancement (resolution, light, color balance); import satellite images; edit terrain meshes; generate contours; slope analysis; volume calculation; export to Google Earth and SketchUp. — [CAD-Earth features (via search snippet)](https://cad-earth.com/features.html)
- Pricing (seen 2026-09-28): Basic $149/year or $14.90/month ("essential tools for image import and basic georeferencing"); Plus/Premium $249/year or $24.90/month ("advanced terrain tools, terrain mesh import & editing, and contour lines"; 15% saving on annual). — [CAD-Earth Buy Now (via search snippet)](https://cad-earth.com/buy-now.html)
- Trial: free 30-day trial. — [CAD-Earth downloads (via search snippet)](https://www.cad-earth.com/downloads.html)
- Supported platforms: "works with AutoCAD Full 2018-2026 (including Civil 3D, Map, and other vertical products)"; requires Windows 10/11 64-bit; "does not work on Mac, Revit, or AutoCAD LT." — [CAD-Earth (via search snippet)](https://cad-earth.com/); Civil 3D listing on Autodesk App Store: [link](https://apps.autodesk.com/CIV3D/en/Detail/Index?id=9157380413877632903&ln=en&os=Win32_64)
- Version history seen on third-party download sites: CAD-Earth 7.0.8 for AutoCAD 2021–2024; 6.1.9 for AutoCAD 2017–2021. — [CivilNode listing](https://civilnode.com/download-software/10111758521415/Arqcom-CAD-Earth-7-0-8-for-AutoCAD-2021-2024), [CivilNode listing](https://civilnode.com/download-software/10123633595874/Arqcom-CAD-Earth-6-1-9-for-AutoCAD-2017-2021)
- Community usage: an Autodesk forum thread "How to open contours from Google Earth" is among the top results for this product class. — [Autodesk Community thread](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/autocad-forum/how-to-open-contours-from-google-earth/td-p/7218321)

### Inferences
- "Calculating point elevations from Google Earth" implies CAD-Earth reads elevations out of the Google Earth desktop application (or its terrain), which is why the Cesium terrain server is offered as an alternative — Cesium World Terrain is itself a blend of public DEMs (SRTM/NED-class in the US), so terrain fidelity is similar to Plex-Earth's default, not lidar.
- No USGS-1 m import path was found in CAD-Earth's materials, so for a US parcel Plex-Earth's custom-GeoTIFF workflow or a QGIS/Equator path is the way to get lidar-grade contours.

### Gaps
- Whether CAD-Earth requires Google Earth Pro desktop to be installed (older versions did) and how it obtains Google elevations in 2026 — not confirmed.
- Any statement from Arqcom on Google terms-of-service compliance — none found.
- Current version number for the 2026 build — not retrieved.

## Key Question 3: Spatial Manager for AutoCAD / BricsCAD / ZWCAD — background maps, terrain/DEM, LiDAR, pricing

### Takeaway
Spatial Manager is a GIS-data plugin rather than a "Google Earth" tool: it draws live background maps (Google Maps, OSM, Bing, Mapbox, Ordnance Survey, WMS/WMTS/COG), and its Terrain wizard builds terrain objects and contours from your own 3D data or from online elevation services; prices start around $345 for the Basic edition, but Professional-edition pricing was not obtainable online at research time.

### Cited Findings
- Purpose: "a powerful AutoCAD plug-in designed for AutoCAD users who need to import, export, transform, and manage spatial data in a simple, fast, and inexpensive way." — [Spatial Manager for AutoCAD overview (via search snippet)](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spm-forautocad/overview/)
- Background maps: "dynamic background maps from many providers such as Google Maps, OpenStreetMap, Bing, MapBox, Ordnance Survey, and customized maps from TMS, WMS/OGC API Maps or WMTS/OGC API Tiles servers or connections to Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG TIFF) type origins." — [Background maps feature page (via search snippet)](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spm-forautocad/features/background-maps/)
- Terrain: "The Spatial Manager Terrain wizard allows AutoCAD users to create Terrains and related objects from 3D data (Points, Meshes, etc.) or from online Elevation services." — [Terrain wizard feature page (via search snippet)](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spm-forautocad/features/terrains/)
- AutoCAD 2026 compatibility announced. — [Blog: Spatial Manager for AutoCAD 2026 compatible](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spatial-manager-for-autocad-2026-compatible/)
- Pricing: Basic edition "starts at $345"; Professional-edition buy page states that "due to maintenance work, it is not possible to obtain prices for this product online" and directs buyers to support@spatialmanager.com. — [Basic edition pricing (via search snippet)](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spm-forautocad/buy/bas/), [Professional edition pricing (via search snippet)](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spm-forautocad/buy/pro/), [estimated price list blog](https://www.spatialmanager.com/spatial-manager-products-estimated-price-list/)
- Listed on Autodesk Marketplace. — [Autodesk Marketplace listing](https://marketplace.autodesk.com/apps/4f94973a-1899-4acb-9076-823b478c0e74)

### Inferences
- Spatial Manager's "online elevation services" are public DEM services, so its terrain is comparable to other web-elevation tools; its strength for a US parcel is loading county parcel shapefiles/GeoJSON and USGS DEM/LAS data you download yourself and turning them into AutoCAD contours in the drawing's coordinate system.

### Gaps

- Professional/Standard edition prices and whether licenses are perpetual or subscription — not obtainable.

## Key Question 4: Autodesk's own tools — AutoCAD Geographic Location / GEOMAP, Civil 3D, Map 3D

### Takeaway
Natively, AutoCAD and its verticals only stream Bing Maps imagery (aerial/road/hybrid) into a georeferenced drawing and let you capture it as a GEOMAPIMAGE raster at Bing-limited resolution; there is no built-in online terrain or building download, and Civil 3D lost its Google Earth surface import after the 2012 release for legal reasons — which is exactly the gap Plex-Earth/CAD-Earth fill.

### Cited Findings
- GEOMAP: "displays online maps using Bing map data. The map can display satellite imagery, vector imagery, or a hybrid view that overlays satellite images over vector images and highlights roads." — [AutoCAD 2025 Help: GEOMAP (via search snippet)](https://help.autodesk.com/view/ACD/2025/ENU/?guid=GUID-DEF7EA7B-6A4B-4520-9A6F-08A944F78218)
- Capture: use Geolocation ribbon "Capture Area" or "Capture Viewport" to create "a new object type called GEOMAPIMAGE or 'Map Image'"; "A captured image defaults to an 'optimal' resolution... you can change the resolution to something higher... The image resolution is limited by Bing, not AutoCAD." — [IMAGINiT: AutoCAD Bing Image Service (via search snippet)](https://resources.imaginit.com/civil-solutions-blog/autodesk-construction-cloud-series-autocad-bing-image-service-geolocation), [Hagerman blog (via search snippet)](https://blog.hagerman.com/2015/08/04/picture-perfect-inserting-and-plotting-bing-imagery/)
- Prerequisite: "You need a georeferenced drawing with an assigned coordinate system, then go to the Geolocation tab on the ribbon and choose the Online Map type"; the Bing service "has been a function for the AutoCAD line of products for about a decade." — [IMAGINiT (via search snippet)](https://resources.imaginit.com/civil-solutions-blog/autodesk-construction-cloud-series-autocad-bing-image-service-geolocation); Autodesk support how-to: [How to display Bing satellite imagery in AutoCAD products](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Importing-Bing-images-into-AutoCAD-Map-3D-and-Civil-3D.html); NRCS step-by-step for Civil 3D: [NRCS Iowa PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/HowTo-%20Geolocation%20w%20Online%20Bing%20Map%20C3D.pdf)
- Known issue: Bing map can appear in the wrong location if the drawing's coordinate system is mis-set. — [Autodesk support article](https://www.autodesk.com/support/technical/article/caas/sfdcarticles/sfdcarticles/Bing-Maps-appear-in-the-wrong-location-in-Civil-3D.html)
- Civil 3D Google Earth import removed: "After the 2012 version, Google Earth surface creation was not included in Civil 3D due to some legal issue... in Civil 3D 2013 there is no longer the option to import from or export to Google Earth." — [Civil 3D Plus blog, 2012 (via search snippet)](https://civil3dplus.wordpress.com/2012/04/30/alternative-to-importing-google-earth-in-civil-3d/); Autodesk forum thread "Surface from Google earth" points users to Plex-Earth. — [Autodesk Community (via search snippet)](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/civil-3d-forum/surface-from-google-earth/td-p/7883462)
- Community question "IS THERE A FREE WAY TO IMPORT TERRAIN DATA?" exists in the Civil 3D forum (answers not retrievable). — [Autodesk Community](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/civil-3d-forum/is-there-a-free-way-to-import-terrain-data/td-p/3790392)

### Inferences
- For a single parcel, plain AutoCAD's Geolocation gives a georeferenced Bing aerial good enough for a base image, but no elevations; Civil 3D users get terrain by importing a downloaded USGS DEM/LAS (standard Civil 3D surface-from-DEM/point-cloud workflow) or via Plex-Earth/CAD-Earth.
- Autodesk's InfraWorks (Model Builder: Bing imagery + OSM buildings + public terrain) and Autodesk Forma (OSM buildings + terrain) are the Autodesk-native routes to 3D building context, but their data-source and export specifics could not be verified in this session (see Gaps).

- InfraWorks Model Builder and Autodesk Forma data sources/resolution/export to Civil 3D or Revit — searches not possible after budget exhaustion; report writer should verify before citing.

## Key Question 5: SketchUp — Add Location (Trimble) and PlaceMaker, plus SketchUp → DWG export

### Takeaway
SketchUp's built-in Add Location gives a geolocated aerial snapshot plus a terrain mesh (provider-dependent resolution; Nearmap high-res imagery is a pay-per-tile add-on for Pro/Studio at roughly $0.04 per 256-px tile with a 200-tile minimum), while the PlaceMaker extension ($109/yr or $20 per 100 credits) is the tool that actually brings in 3D building massing (OSM plus a ~200-million-building US footprint dataset), roads, water, trees, Nearmap/Bluesky/Mapbox aerials, and — for subscribers — Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles mesh via the official Map Tiles API. SketchUp Pro then exports the whole site model to DWG/DXF.

### Cited Findings
- Add Location purpose: "add depth, layers, and dimensions to their 3D models using 2D and 3D terrain maps, aerial imagery, and precise shadows." — [Trimble Add Location product page (via search snippet)](https://sketchup.trimble.com/en/products/add-location)
- Nearmap in Add Location: available since SketchUp 2020.1 via File > Geo-Location > Add Location; "Nearmap provides high resolution aerial imagery and 3D reality mesh that can be imported into SketchUp Pro." — [Nearmap Help Center: Nearmap and SketchUp (via search snippet)](https://help.nearmap.com/kb/articles/1217-nearmap-and-sketchup)
- Nearmap cost model: "available for purchase on an as-needed basis when you have an active SketchUp Pro or SketchUp Studio subscription or a Classic License for SketchUp 2020"; "roughly $0.04 per tile (tiles are 256×256 pixel images) and is subject to a 200 tile minimum"; coverage "for all major cities and suburbs in the U.S., Canada, Australia, and New Zealand." — [SketchUp Community announcement thread, 2020 (via search snippet)](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/nearmap-aerial-imagery-is-now-available-in-sketchup-s-add-location/132811)
- Terrain quality note from that thread: "Nearmap areas can provide more accurate 3D Meshes and terrain." — [SketchUp Community (via search snippet)](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/nearmap-aerial-imagery-is-now-available-in-sketchup-s-add-location/132811/59)
- Terrain tiles can be missing "because those sections weren't available at the resolution selected or the provider didn't get the data for that area—to fix this, try importing again using a different resolution or provider." — [SketchUp Help: Troubleshooting Add Location (via search snippet)](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup/troubleshooting-add-location)
- Trimble announced a "substantial upgrade to Add Location" (2024-era thread; details not retrievable). — [SketchUp Community thread](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/weve-made-a-substantial-upgrade-to-add-location/206394)

- PlaceMaker building data: "imports 3D buildings from around the world with universal coverage for the United States (over 200 million buildings for the US alone). It imports OpenStreetMap 3D roads, walks, paths, water and 3D building massing models, and PlaceMaker uses Nearmap, Bluesky Hexagon and Mapbox for high resolution aerial imagery." — [PlaceMaker (via search snippet)](https://www.suplacemaker.com/sketchup-placemaker/)
- PlaceMaker Google 3D: "Google 3D Tile import is available for active SketchUp PlaceMaker subscribers only and will not work for Pay-As-You-Go customers or if your subscription license has expired. This feature also requires SketchUp Pro Desktop version 2022 or newer." API key must have the Map Tiles API enabled; "Google provides a generous free tier for their data, but a payment method is required." — [mind.sight.studios support: API key and Google Earth 3D Tiles (via search snippet)](https://support.mindsightstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/24330438654995-How-to-setup-your-API-key-and-import-Google-Earth-3D-Tiles); Design8 how-to for PlaceMaker 3.3: [link](https://design8.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/47001263589-use-placemaker-3-3-for-importing-3d-tiles-from-google-earth)

- Tutorials: The SketchUp Essentials maintains a PlaceMaker tutorial series. — [thesketchupessentials.com](https://www.thesketchupessentials.com/placemakertutorials)

Move far-from-origin CAD geometry near the origin when importing civil plans. — [SketchUp Help: Importing and Exporting CAD Files (via search snippet)](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup/importing-and-exporting-cad-files), [SketchUp Community: Export 3D model to .dwg](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/export-3d-model-to-dwg-format/149653)

### Inferences
- The "200 million US buildings" figure matches the scale of Microsoft's open US Building Footprints dataset (~130M+) or Overture/Google Open Buildings-type datasets; PlaceMaker's US building coverage is therefore footprint-plus-estimated-height massing, not photogrammetry, unless the Google 3D Tiles option is used.
- Google 3D Tiles imported by PlaceMaker are a textured photogrammetry mesh — good for visual context, poor for editing, and their downstream use is governed by Google Maps Platform terms (see KQ 9).
- For the user's parcel-plus-new-homes goal, SketchUp Pro + PlaceMaker is the cheapest path to a textured terrain + neighboring building masses + Nearmap aerial that also exports cleanly to DWG for AutoCAD.

## Key Question 8: Free QGIS workflow — lidar DEM + parcels + imagery → contours and footprints to DXF

### Takeaway
QGIS (native LAS/LAZ tools since 3.32, June 2023) can turn a free USGS 3DEP lidar tile into smoothed 1 ft/0.5 m contours and export them as DXF with Z values for AutoCAD, and can export parcel/OSM building polygons the same way; the main pitfalls are converting geometry to 2.5D (LineString25D) before export so Z survives, and smoothing/simplifying lidar contours so they are not jagged. US 3DEP 1 m lidar coverage is broad and expanding, with a stated ~0.53 m RMSE for the dynamic elevation service.

### Cited Findings
- Native lidar: "Since Version 3.32 (June 2023), QGIS has had native LiDAR tools, with the basic workflow bringing the LAS/LAZ file into QGIS, then 'Export to raster' to create a TIF file"; then Raster > Extraction > Contour. — [RPLS forum: generating contours from lidar (via search snippet)](https://rpls.com/forums/photogrammetry-lidar-uas/help-generating-contours-from-lidar-data/), [mjk2.net: Lidar contours with QGIS](http://www.mjk2.net/waffle/misc/Lidar/lidar.htm)
- Quality steps: "resampling using SAGA Resampling filter is recommended to produce smooth contour lines"; "simplify them using the Simplify filter"; then "Export >> Export Features As, and pick Autocad DXF." — [Map Hub Online: QGIS contour lines with Z to AutoCAD DXF (via search snippet)](https://maphub.online/qgis-to-autocad-data-export/)
- Z preservation: "shapefile geometry is in 2D vector mode, but the geometry type should change to Line(LineString25D) before exporting to preserve Z-coordinates." — [Geodose: export SHP to CAD without losing elevation (via search snippet)](https://www.geodose.com/2019/10/how-to-export-shp-to-cad-with-elevation.html); video walkthrough: [YouTube: 3D contours in QGIS exported to CAD with labels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGBsgsP7304)
- USGS 3DEP resolution/coverage: "The 1-meter dataset was introduced in 2015 with limited coverage of the U.S., but is expanding as new DEMs from 3DEP quality level 2 or better lidar data are acquired"; the seamless 1 m product has been in production "since mid-2025, with new tiles added as they become available." — [USGS: coverage of 3DEP DEMs (via search snippet)](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-coverage-3d-elevation-program-3dep-dems), [USGS: About 3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/about-3dep-products-services)
- USGS 3DEP accuracy: 1/3 arc-second seamless DEM "approximately 0.82 meters root mean square error (RMSE)" (as of 2022, vs ~25,000 NGS OPUS points); the dynamic elevation service including 1 m lidar DEMs "currently has a RMSE of 0.53 meters," varying significantly across the U.S. — [USGS FAQ: vertical accuracy of seamless 3DEP DEMs (via search snippet)](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-vertical-accuracy-seamless-3dep-dems), [USGS FAQ](https://usgs.gov/faqs/what-vertical-accuracy-3d-elevation-program-3dep-dems)
- For contrast, Google Earth elevations: a peer-reviewed study found "mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) of GE roadway elevation error are 1.32 meters and 2.27 meters," and Google itself says measurements "are estimates, may not be 100% accurate, and are not substitutes for onsite or survey-grade measurements." — [ETASR: Vertical Accuracy of Google Earth Data (via search snippet)](https://www.etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/7121), [Google Earth docs: Measure distances and areas (via search snippet)](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/earth/measure-distances?authuser=7), discussion: [MavicPilots thread](https://mavicpilots.com/threads/how-good-is-google-earth-pro-for-ground-elevation-changes.35697/)
- Point-cloud-to-CAD services exist as an alternative to DIY (e.g., LidarVisor "Convert LiDAR to DXF"). — [LidarVisor](https://lidarvisor.com/point-cloud-to-cad/)

### Inferences
- For one US parcel, the QGIS route yields the same lidar-grade contours as Equator at zero cost, at the expense of a few hours' learning; parcels (county GIS shapefile) and building footprints (OSM/Microsoft) export to DXF from the same project, giving AutoCAD a georeferenced 2D base plus 3D polylines.
- Because 3DEP RMSE is ~0.5 m and lidar DTMs are bare-earth, contours at 1 ft interval are plausible for concept grading and tiny-home pad placement but still not a substitute for a boundary/topo survey for permits.

### Gaps
- No retrievable step-by-step for exporting OSM/Microsoft building footprints with height attributes from QGIS to DXF as extruded 3D solids (DXF export flattens attributes) — only 2D polylines/3D polylines are documented.
